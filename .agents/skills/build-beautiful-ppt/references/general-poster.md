# Poster and infographic branch

Use this branch only for a poster, infographic, or highly visual one-page or few-page deliverable.

## Direction selection

Form two or three substantially different visual directions before implementation. Vary at least two dimensions: composition, visual medium, typography, palette, information hierarchy, or image treatment. Compare recognizability, thumbnail impact, information capacity, editability, and coexistence of text and visuals. Select one direction and record why it best serves the communication job.

## Visual medium

- Use real or official photography for people, products, places, facilities, events, interfaces, and physical evidence when available.
- Use generated imagery for conceptual or atmospheric needs when appropriate and permitted.
- Use native editable shapes and connectors for simple structure, hierarchy, process, causality, comparison, and spatial relationships.
- Do not default to a row of rounded boxes and thin arrows merely because it is easy to implement.
- Do not force unrelated imagery into the composition to meet a quantity target.

## Composition

Establish one dominant focal point, a clear reading path, and controlled secondary detail. Reserve enough negative space to separate semantic groups without leaving planned regions empty. Make labels, legends, and source marks readable at the intended output size.

Keep text, charts, shapes, connectors, and labels editable whenever the selected backend supports them. Use a full-slide bitmap only when the user requests a flat image or editability is impossible and explicitly disclosed.

## Review

Inspect the output at full size and as a thumbnail. Check focus, reading order, contrast, image crops, overflow, overlap, source attribution, and whether the main visual actually communicates the core idea.
